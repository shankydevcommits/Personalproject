# Moving-In Condition Report & Rights Checklist — New South Wales
### Get this right on day one — it protects your bond for the entire tenancy.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only, not legal advice.*

---

## Section 1 — Before You Sign the Lease

- [ ] **Confirm you're being given the standard form agreement** — mandatory under Schedule 1 of the Residential Tenancies Regulation 2019 (current version from 19 May 2025). Agents/landlords cannot substitute their own non-compliant version.
- [ ] **Confirm no extra fees have been charged to get to this point** — background check or application fees have been banned since 31 October 2024.
- [ ] **Ask about smoke alarm compliance** — landlords must ensure smoke alarms are installed and maintained in working order; ask when they were last checked if this isn't already disclosed.
- [ ] **If you have a pet, submit your request via the NSW Fair Trading Pet Application Form (Part A)** as early as possible — the landlord has 21 days to respond or the request is automatically approved, and they cannot charge extra rent or bond for it.

## Section 2 — The Condition Report Itself

- [ ] **Request the Schedule 2 condition report** if not automatically provided.
- [ ] **Complete your own section thoroughly**, room by room, rather than relying solely on the agent's version.
- [ ] **Photograph everything**, especially existing wear, damage, or imperfections — timestamped photos are your strongest evidence later.
- [ ] **Note existing issues explicitly** — scuffs, stains, worn carpet, anything not in perfect condition.
- [ ] **Test everything that should work**: taps, toilets, switches, power points, stovetop, and any included appliances.
- [ ] **Submit your condition report within the required timeframe** set out in your agreement.
- [ ] **Keep a copy for yourself** — you'll need it again at the end of your lease.

## Section 3 — Ongoing Rights During Your Tenancy

- [ ] **Know your rent increase protections.** Rent can only be increased **once every 12 months**, and landlords must give at least **60 days' written notice**. There's no cap on the increase amount itself in NSW, but you can challenge an excessive increase at NCAT.
- [ ] **Know that no-grounds evictions are banned.** Since 19 May 2025, your landlord must have a valid, documented reason to end your tenancy (e.g. sale requiring vacant possession, significant renovation, the landlord or a family member moving in, or a genuine breach) — and must provide a Termination Information Statement with supporting evidence.
- [ ] **Know you can challenge a termination notice.** If you believe the stated reason isn't genuine, you can challenge its validity at NCAT — you have **90 days** from receiving the notice to apply.
- [ ] **Understand renovation-related terminations require extra documentation** — since 20 June 2025, landlords ending a tenancy for significant renovations must provide specific supporting documents, not just a stated intention.
- [ ] **Know rent payment methods can't disadvantage you unfairly** — Centrepay became a mandatory payment option for eligible tenants from 2 March 2026.

## Section 4 — Red Flags at Move-In

- [ ] **A condition report with suspiciously few noted issues** on an older or clearly lived-in property.
- [ ] **Being pressured to sign before receiving the condition report or properly inspecting the property.**
- [ ] **Any request for fees beyond what's legally permitted** to secure the tenancy.
- [ ] **Vague or unsubstantiated pet refusals** — landlords must give a reason from the prescribed list, not a blanket "no pets" policy.
