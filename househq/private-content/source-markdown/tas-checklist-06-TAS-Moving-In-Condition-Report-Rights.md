# Moving-In Condition Report & Rights Checklist — Tasmania
### Get this right on day one — it protects your bond for the entire tenancy.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only, not legal advice.*

---

## Section 1 — Before You Sign the Lease

- [ ] **Know your tenancy is governed by the Residential Tenancy Act 1997 (Tas)**, with minimum standards under Part 3B and smoke alarm requirements under Part 3A of the Act, supported by the Residential Tenancy (Smoke Alarms) Regulations 2022.
- [ ] **Ask about smoke alarm compliance** and any recent safety checks — this is a specifically regulated area under Tasmanian tenancy law.
- [ ] **If you have a pet, know consent is required by default** — Tasmania doesn't have an automatic pet-approval right like some other states; you'll need to seek landlord consent, though they cannot apply a blanket refusal unreasonably.

## Section 2 — The Condition Report Itself

- [ ] **Request the entry condition report** if not automatically provided.
- [ ] **Complete your own section thoroughly**, room by room.
- [ ] **Photograph everything**, especially existing wear, damage, or imperfections.
- [ ] **Note existing issues explicitly.**
- [ ] **Test everything that should work**: taps, toilets, switches, power points, stovetop, and any included appliances.
- [ ] **Submit your condition report within the required timeframe.**
- [ ] **Keep a copy for yourself** — you'll need it again at the end of your lease for the joint MyBond claim process.

## Section 3 — Ongoing Rights During Your Tenancy

- [ ] **Know your rent increase protections.** Rent increases follow a **60-day notice** requirement and a **12-month rule** limiting frequency — if you believe an increase is unreasonable, you can seek a re-determination through the Magistrates Court process.
- [ ] **Understand Tasmania's termination position sits between other states.** For **periodic tenancies**, there's no bare "no reason" ground — similar to NSW and SA. However, for **fixed-term tenancies**, a notice tied only to the end of the term (without needing a substantive reason) remains available under section 42(1)(d) — functioning similarly to how Western Australia's rules currently work for fixed-term endings.
- [ ] **Know about urgent repairs.** For genuinely urgent issues (no hot water, gas leak, sewage problems, etc.), you may be able to arrange repairs yourself after attempting to contact your landlord, and seek reimbursement up to specified caps.
- [ ] **Request repairs in writing** — this creates a paper trail if a dispute later needs to go to the Residential Tenancy Commissioner.

## Section 4 — Red Flags at Move-In

- [ ] **A condition report with suspiciously few noted issues** on an older or clearly lived-in property.
- [ ] **Being pressured to sign before receiving the condition report or properly inspecting.**
- [ ] **Signs the property fails minimum standards or smoke alarm requirements.**
- [ ] **A blanket "no pets" refusal without genuine consideration of your request.**

## Quick Reference — TAS Tenant Rights & Key Facts

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Governing legislation | Residential Tenancy Act 1997 (Tas) |
| Minimum standards | Part 3B of the Act |
| Smoke alarms | Part 3A + Residential Tenancy (Smoke Alarms) Regulations 2022 |
| Rent increase | 60-day notice, 12-month rule |
| Periodic tenancy termination | No bare "no reason" ground |
| Fixed-term termination | Notice tied to term expiry available without stated reason (s42(1)(d)) |
| Pet disputes | TASCAT, since 20 March 2026 |
