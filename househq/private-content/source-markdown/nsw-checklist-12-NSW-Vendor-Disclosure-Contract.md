# Vendor Disclosure & Contract Checklist — New South Wales
### Your obligations as a seller — get this wrong and buyers can walk away, even after exchange.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only, not legal advice. Your Contract of Sale must be prepared by a solicitor or licensed conveyancer — this checklist helps you gather what they'll need, not replace their advice.*

---

## Section 1 — Understanding Your Disclosure Obligation

- [ ] **Know the Contract of Sale must be prepared before your property is listed** — in NSW, this is the vendor's obligation, not something assembled after a buyer is found.
- [ ] **Understand your solicitor or conveyancer prepares the contract**, but you are responsible for the accuracy of the underlying information you provide to them.
- [ ] **Know the consequences of an inadequate contract.** Missing or defective disclosure can allow a buyer to rescind or create grounds for dispute after exchange — get it right from the outset rather than treating this as a formality.

## Section 2 — Information to Gather for Your Solicitor/Conveyancer

- [ ] **Title details** — current title search.
- [ ] **s10.7 planning certificate** — obtained from council, disclosing zoning, heritage listings, flood/bushfire risk, and any known planning restrictions.
- [ ] **Mortgages and charges** on the property.
- [ ] **Easements, covenants and restrictions**, both registered and any you're aware of.
- [ ] **Home Building Compensation Fund (HBCF) certificate**, if residential building work over $20,000 has been carried out in the past 6 years.
- [ ] **Sewer service diagram** and details of connected services.
- [ ] **Strata records**, if applicable — recent AGM minutes, financials, sinking fund details, and known disputes or special levies.
- [ ] **Any known defects or issues** you're aware of, even if not immediately obvious — full and frank disclosure protects you from post-settlement disputes.

## Section 3 — Underquoting & Price Transparency Obligations

- [ ] **Understand your agent's legal obligation.** Under the Property and Stock Agents Act 2002, your agent must provide a genuine, reasonable estimated selling price in your agency agreement, supported by comparable sales, and must revise it as required — this is a legal obligation on the agent, not just good practice.
- [ ] **Don't ask your agent to advertise a misleadingly low price to generate interest.** Beyond the ethical issue, this is precisely the conduct underquoting protections (maximum penalty **$22,000**) are designed to prevent, and it creates real risk for both you and your agent.
- [ ] **Keep records of price guide discussions with your agent** — the agency agreement should document the estimated price and any revisions.

## Section 4 — Auction-Specific Considerations

- [ ] **Understand there's no cooling-off period for the buyer once the hammer falls** at auction — ensure your Contract of Sale is genuinely finalised well before auction day.
- [ ] **Confirm your agent's auction marketing complies with underquoting rules**, including the price guide displayed.

## Section 5 — Final Checks Before Going to Market

- [ ] **Confirm your Contract of Sale is complete and reviewed by your solicitor** before your first open home.
- [ ] **Cross-check the contract against your own knowledge of the property** — flag any inconsistency with your solicitor immediately.
- [ ] **Keep a copy of everything provided to buyers** — useful protection if a dispute arises after settlement.

## Quick Reference — NSW Vendor Disclosure Essentials

| Requirement | Key detail |
|---|---|
| Contract of Sale | Prepared by vendor's solicitor/conveyancer, before listing |
| s10.7 planning certificate | Zoning, heritage, flood/bushfire risk disclosure |
| HBCF certificate | Required for building work over $20,000 in past 6 years |
| Underquoting prohibition | Maximum penalty $22,000, governed by Property and Stock Agents Act 2002 |
| Auction cooling-off | None, for either party, once the hammer falls |
