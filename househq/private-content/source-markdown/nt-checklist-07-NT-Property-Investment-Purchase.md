# Property Investment Purchase Checklist — Northern Territory
### Due diligence for buying an investment property in Australia's most tax-friendly jurisdiction for landholding.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only — not financial, tax or legal advice. Engage a qualified accountant, mortgage broker, and conveyancer before purchasing.*

---

## Section 1 — Structuring the Purchase (The NT's Genuine Advantage)

- [ ] **Understand the NT's standout feature: no land tax at all.** The Northern Territory is the **only Australian jurisdiction with no general land tax** — investors pay zero annual land tax on rental properties, a genuinely significant structural advantage over every state and the ACT for ongoing holding costs.
- [ ] **Understand the NT also has no foreign buyer duty surcharge.** Along with the ACT lacking a land tax surcharge for some categories, the NT is unique in having **neither a foreign buyer stamp duty surcharge nor a land tax surcharge** — foreign buyers pay the same standard rates as Australian citizens.
- [ ] **Still decide on ownership structure carefully** — even without land tax considerations, individual, joint, trust, or company structures carry different income tax and asset-protection implications.
- [ ] **Understand you'll pay standard transfer duty as an investor** — the NT's formula-based/tiered duty system applies to all buyers, with no investor-specific concession or exemption (the same as first home buyers pay, since the NT doesn't offer FHB duty relief either).
- [ ] **Check whether the House and Land Package Exemption (HLPE) could apply to your purchase**, if you're building rather than buying established — this isn't restricted to first home buyers, and gives a full duty waiver on eligible single-contract packages until 30 June 2027.
- [ ] **Get investment-specific finance pre-approval.**

## Section 2 — Due Diligence on the Property Itself

- [ ] **Order a building and pest inspection within your cooling-off window.**
- [ ] **Have a conveyancer or solicitor review the contract before you sign.**
- [ ] **Pay particular attention to cyclone/storm resilience** if purchasing in Darwin or the Top End — this affects both insurance costs and long-term maintenance budgeting.
- [ ] **Research the local rental market specifically** — vacancy rates, comparable rents, and tenant demand, noting the NT's rental market is significantly influenced by defence force postings, resource sector cycles, and government employment.

## Section 3 — Setting Up for Compliance From Day One

- [ ] **Budget for compliance with the Residential Tenancies Act 1999**, including the rent-bidding/gazumping ban and the specific rent increase conditions.
- [ ] **Decide on self-management vs. a property manager** — genuinely worth weighing given the NT's distinctive tenant population (higher turnover in some markets due to postings/contracts) and climate-related maintenance demands.
- [ ] **Arrange landlord insurance**, specifically checking cyclone/storm coverage given the NT's climate.
- [ ] **Engage a quantity surveyor for a tax depreciation schedule** shortly after settlement — see the separate Tax & Depreciation Checklist in this series.

## Section 4 — Understanding the Current Tax Environment

- [ ] **Be aware Commonwealth CGT settings for investment property are changing**, with reforms affecting the CGT discount generally applying from **1 July 2027** — confirm the finalised current position with your accountant.
- [ ] **Get advice on purchase and sale timing relative to this changeover** if your investment horizon spans the 2027 transition.

## Quick Reference — NT Investment Property Snapshot (July 2026)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Land tax | **None** — the only Australian jurisdiction without one |
| Foreign buyer surcharge (duty) | **None** |
| Foreign buyer land tax surcharge | Not applicable (no land tax) |
| Standard transfer duty | Formula-based under $525,000; tiered flat rates above |
| HLPE duty waiver | Available for eligible house-and-land packages, until 30 June 2027 |

*Duty and grant settings change with territory budgets — always confirm current figures with the Territory Revenue Office before settling on a purchase strategy.*
