# Property Investment Purchase Checklist — Queensland
### Due diligence for buying an investment property, beyond what a homebuyer checklist covers.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only — not financial, tax or legal advice. Engage a qualified accountant, mortgage broker, and conveyancer before purchasing.*

---

## Section 1 — Structuring the Purchase

- [ ] **Decide on ownership structure before you buy** — individual, joint, trust, or company each carry different land tax, income tax, and asset-protection implications.
- [ ] **Understand Queensland land tax thresholds.** The threshold is **$600,000 for individuals** and **$350,000 for companies and trusts** — meaning trust/company structures are caught by land tax at a much lower value than individual ownership.
- [ ] **Understand the absentee owner surcharge.** Absentee individuals face a land tax surcharge (currently **3%**, having stepped up from an original 2%) on the value above the relevant threshold.
- [ ] **If you're a foreign entity, understand Additional Foreign Acquirer Duty (AFAD).** Foreign acquirers pay an additional **7% duty** on top of standard transfer duty for residential land — separate from any land tax foreign surcharge that applies annually while you hold the property.
- [ ] **Know that Queensland's foreign surcharge relief framework changed significantly for 2026–27.** From land tax liabilities arising on or after 30 June 2026, the previous ex gratia relief system for the land tax foreign surcharge is being replaced with a new administrative exemption framework, requiring new application forms from 1 July 2026 if you're seeking relief as a genuine commercial contributor or residential land developer — note that a foreign entity simply holding land passively as a landlord/investor generally does **not** qualify for this relief.
- [ ] **Get investment-specific finance pre-approval.**

## Section 2 — Due Diligence on the Property Itself

- [ ] **Order a building and pest inspection** — just as essential for an investment purchase.
- [ ] **Review the Seller Disclosure Statement (Form 2) and prescribed certificates carefully** — mandatory since 1 August 2025, and just as relevant to your due diligence as an investor as it is for owner-occupiers.
- [ ] **If buying a body corporate property, review the records thoroughly** — sinking fund health, special levies, and any disputes directly affect your investment returns.
- [ ] **Research the local rental market specifically** — vacancy rates, comparable rents, and tenant demand for your target suburb.
- [ ] **Factor in climate-specific risks** — flood mapping, cyclone exposure, and insurance cost implications, which can materially affect both your ongoing costs and future resale value in parts of Queensland.

## Section 3 — Setting Up for Compliance From Day One

- [ ] **Budget for Minimum Housing Standards compliance** if the property needs work to meet the mandatory categories (weatherproofing, locks, plumbing, window coverings, safe fixtures, functional kitchen/laundry) — factor this into your purchase budget.
- [ ] **Decide on self-management vs. a property manager** — QLD's compliance requirements (Form 22 applications, minimum housing standards, QCAT processes, bond rules) are genuinely complex for a self-managing landlord unfamiliar with the RTRA Act.
- [ ] **Arrange landlord insurance**, separate from standard building insurance.
- [ ] **Engage a quantity surveyor for a tax depreciation schedule** shortly after settlement — see the separate Tax & Depreciation Checklist in this series.

## Section 4 — Understanding the Current Tax Environment

- [ ] **Be aware Commonwealth CGT settings for investment property are changing**, with reforms affecting the CGT discount generally applying from 1 July 2027 — confirm the finalised current position with your accountant.
- [ ] **Get advice on purchase and sale timing relative to this changeover** if your investment horizon spans the 2027 transition.

## Quick Reference — QLD Investment Property Snapshot (July 2026)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Land tax threshold (individuals) | $600,000 |
| Land tax threshold (companies/trusts) | $350,000 |
| Absentee owner surcharge | 3% (stepped up from 2%) |
| Additional Foreign Acquirer Duty (AFAD) | 7%, on top of standard transfer duty |
| Land tax foreign surcharge relief | New administrative exemption framework from 30 June 2026 |
| Land tax assessment | Based on holdings at 30 June each year |

*Land tax and surcharge rates change with state budgets — always confirm current figures with the Queensland Revenue Office before settling on a purchase strategy.*
