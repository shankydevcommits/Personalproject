# Tax & Depreciation Essentials Checklist — Investors (Northern Territory)
### What to have in place — and why the NT is structurally different from every other Australian jurisdiction.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only — this is NOT financial, tax or legal advice. Always engage a qualified accountant or tax agent for advice specific to your situation.*

---

## Section 1 — Records to Keep From Day One

- [ ] **A tax depreciation schedule from a qualified quantity surveyor** — Division 43 (building) and Division 40 (plant and equipment) deductions. These are federal (Commonwealth) tax matters, so they apply in the NT exactly as they do everywhere else in Australia.
- [ ] **Receipts for repairs vs. improvements — kept separately.** Repairs are often immediately deductible; improvements form part of your CGT cost base.
- [ ] **Stamp duty receipts.**
- [ ] **Records of your accountant fees and depreciation schedule costs.**

## Section 2 — The NT's Unique State/Territory Tax Position

- [ ] **Understand there's no land tax to budget for.** The Northern Territory is the only Australian state or territory with **no general land tax** — this means your ongoing state-level holding costs are structurally lower than an equivalent property anywhere else in Australia.
- [ ] **Understand there's no foreign buyer surcharge either.** Unlike every state and the ACT, the NT charges **no additional duty surcharge for foreign purchasers** — foreign buyers pay identical rates to Australian citizens.
- [ ] **Don't confuse "no land tax" with "no property-related state taxes at all."** You'll still pay standard transfer (stamp) duty at purchase, calculated via the NT's formula-based system for properties under $525,000 and tiered flat rates above that threshold.
- [ ] **Check whether the House and Land Package Exemption (HLPE) applied to your purchase** if you bought via a single house-and-land contract — this could mean you paid no duty at all, which affects your cost base calculations for CGT purposes later.

## Section 3 — Understanding the Changing CGT Environment (Federal, Applies Everywhere)

- [ ] **Be aware Commonwealth CGT settings for investment property are changing**, with reforms affecting the CGT discount generally applying from **1 July 2027** — this is a federal change and applies to NT property exactly as it does elsewhere in Australia. Confirm the finalised current position with your accountant.
- [ ] **If planning to sell, get advice on timing relative to the 2027 changeover.**

## Section 4 — Depreciation Schedule Timing

- [ ] **Get your depreciation schedule prepared as early as possible after settlement.**
- [ ] **Update it after any renovations or major asset additions.**
- [ ] **Don't assume an older NT property has no deductions available** — plant and equipment deductions can still apply, and given the NT's climate, air conditioning systems are often a particularly significant depreciable asset worth confirming with your quantity surveyor.

## Section 5 — Questions to Bring to Your Accountant

- [ ] Given there's no NT land tax, is my overall investment strategy optimised to take advantage of the lower ongoing holding costs (e.g. does this change my hold-vs-sell calculus compared to a similar property interstate)?
- [ ] Did I benefit from the HLPE duty waiver, and how does that affect my cost base?
- [ ] How will the 2027 CGT changes affect a future sale of this property?
- [ ] Is my depreciation schedule up to date with any renovations or asset purchases since it was last prepared?
