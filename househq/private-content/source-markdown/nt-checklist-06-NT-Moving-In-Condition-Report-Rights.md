# Moving-In Condition Report & Rights Checklist — Northern Territory
### Get this right on day one — it protects your bond for the entire tenancy.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only, not legal advice.*

---

## Section 1 — Before You Sign the Lease

- [ ] **Know your tenancy is governed by the Residential Tenancies Act 1999 (NT).**
- [ ] **Know rent bidding and unilateral rent increases between offer and signing are prohibited** — if the rent you're offered differs from what you agreed at inspection, that's a red flag worth raising.
- [ ] **Ask about cyclone/wet-season preparedness** if you're in Darwin or the Top End — window/door seals, drainage, and general storm readiness are genuinely relevant liveability questions in the NT.

## Section 2 — The Condition Report Itself

- [ ] **Request the entry condition report** if not automatically provided.
- [ ] **Complete your own section thoroughly**, room by room.
- [ ] **Photograph everything**, especially existing wear, damage, or imperfections.
- [ ] **Note existing issues explicitly.**
- [ ] **Test everything that should work**: taps, toilets, switches, power points, stovetop, air conditioning (genuinely important given the NT's climate), and any included appliances.
- [ ] **Submit your condition report within the required timeframe.**
- [ ] **Keep a copy for yourself** — you'll need it again at the end of your lease.

## Section 3 — Ongoing Rights During Your Tenancy

- [ ] **Know the specific conditions required for a rent increase.** All of the following must apply: your tenancy agreement has a condition allowing rent increases; the agreement notes the calculation method; you're given at least **30 days' notice**; the increase doesn't occur within the **first 6 months** of your agreement; and it happens **no more than once every 6 months**.
- [ ] **Know you can challenge an excessive rent increase.** You can apply to NTCAT, which will assess whether the rate is excessive compared to market rent for similar properties.
- [ ] **Know about rent decrease rights too** — if services have changed, or if the rent has become excessive relative to the market, you can negotiate directly with your landlord or apply to the Commissioner of Tenancies for a rental review if negotiations don't work.
- [ ] **Request repairs in writing** — this creates a paper trail if a dispute later needs to go to NTCAT.
- [ ] **Know rent arrears process timing.** If your rent is more than 14 days overdue, your landlord can serve a notice giving you 7 days to pay before applying to NTCAT to end the tenancy.

## Section 4 — Red Flags at Move-In

- [ ] **A condition report with suspiciously few noted issues** on an older or clearly lived-in property.
- [ ] **Being pressured to sign before receiving the condition report or properly inspecting.**
- [ ] **A rent figure different from what was advertised or discussed at inspection** — this may breach the ban on rent bidding/gazumping.
- [ ] **Non-functioning air conditioning or storm-related seals/drainage issues** — genuinely material in the NT's climate.

## Quick Reference — NT Tenant Rights & Key Facts

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Governing legislation | Residential Tenancies Act 1999 (NT) |
| Rent bidding/gazumping | Prohibited |
| Rent increase conditions | Agreement clause required, 30 days' notice, none in first 6 months, max once per 6 months |
| Maximum bond | 4 weeks' rent |
| Rent arrears process | 14 days overdue → 7-day notice → NTCAT application |
| Dispute resolution | NTCAT |
