# End-of-Lease Bond-Back Checklist — Queensland
### Protect your money — most bond disputes are avoidable with the right documentation.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only, not legal advice.*

---

## Section 1 — Understand How Your Bond Works

- [ ] **Know your bond is held by the Residential Tenancies Authority (RTA)**, not your landlord or agent directly.
- [ ] **Know the maximum bond amount.** A managing party cannot request more than **4 weeks' rent** as bond.
- [ ] **Understand bond is not a general maintenance fund** — it can only be claimed against for unpaid rent, cleaning to restore the property (allowing for fair wear and tear), or damage beyond fair wear and tear.
- [ ] **Check out the portable bond pilot if you're moving within South East Queensland.** A portable bond scheme launched as a pilot in January 2026, allowing eligible tenants to transfer their bond to a new property without finding a new bond payment upfront — check current eligibility with the RTA if this could apply to your move.

## Section 2 — Before You Move Out

- [ ] **Locate your original entry condition report (Form 1a)** — your baseline for comparison.
- [ ] **Compare the property's current condition against that report**, room by room.
- [ ] **Clean thoroughly**, addressing anything genuinely beyond fair wear and tear.
- [ ] **Check every item on the original inventory** is present and in working order.
- [ ] **Settle any outstanding rent** before vacating.

## Section 3 — At the Final Inspection

- [ ] **Complete the exit condition report (Form 14a)** together with your landlord/agent where possible.
- [ ] **Take your own timestamped photos** of every room after cleaning, regardless of whether the agent attends.
- [ ] **Keep every receipt** — cleaning, repairs, pest control — as evidence.

## Section 4 — Claiming Your Bond

- [ ] **Lodge your side of the bond claim with the RTA** once you've vacated.
- [ ] **If both parties agree**, the RTA releases the bond according to the agreed split.
- [ ] **If there's a dispute**, either party can apply to the **Queensland Civil and Administrative Tribunal (QCAT)** for resolution.
- [ ] **Know what happens if a bond dispute is dismissed or withdrawn at QCAT** — the RTA will release the bond following the original decision, so understand the implications before withdrawing an application.

## Section 5 — If There's a Dispute

- [ ] **Gather your evidence before applying to QCAT**: entry and exit condition reports, timestamped photos, receipts, and any correspondence.
- [ ] **Apply to QCAT** if you and your landlord can't agree — the tribunal will review evidence and make a binding decision.
- [ ] **Know landlords can seek compensation beyond the bond** if it doesn't cover their costs — this can also go to QCAT, so ensure your own records are thorough regardless of the bond amount itself.
