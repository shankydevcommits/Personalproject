# Landlord Compliance Checklist — Northern Territory
### The obligations every rental provider must meet under the Residential Tenancies Act 1999.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only, not legal advice. Confirm current requirements with Northern Territory Consumer Affairs before relying on this checklist alone.*

---

## Section 1 — Bonds

- [ ] **Respect the maximum bond of 4 weeks' rent.**
- [ ] **Hold bond funds in a designated trust account** if you're an agent, or in trust if you're a private landlord — tenants are entitled to know the bank and account details.
- [ ] **Provide a receipt** for bond paid by cheque, cash, or credit card.
- [ ] **Notify the relevant authority if you'll be away from the NT for more than 14 days** as a private landlord.
- [ ] **Never claim fair wear and tear against a bond.**

## Section 2 — Rent

- [ ] **Only increase rent if all required conditions are met**: your agreement has a rent-increase clause, the calculation method is documented, you give at least **30 days' notice**, the increase doesn't occur in the first **6 months**, and it happens no more than **once every 6 months**.
- [ ] **Never engage in rent bidding, rent auctions, or gazumping** — these are specifically prohibited, along with unilaterally increasing rent between offer and formal agreement.
- [ ] **Follow the correct arrears process**: a 7-day notice once rent is more than 14 days overdue, with an NTCAT application available if the tenant doesn't pay within that window.

## Section 3 — Applications and Privacy

- [ ] **Only gather the most relevant personal information needed to assess an application** — don't over-collect.
- [ ] **Take reasonable steps to protect tenant information**, and destroy it within required timeframes.
- [ ] **Understand tenancy database listing rules** — there are specific circumstances governing when someone can be listed, with a pathway for a victim tenant to apply to NTCAT to have their information removed.

## Section 4 — Condition Reports and Repairs

- [ ] **Complete entry and exit condition reports** for every tenancy.
- [ ] **Respond to repair requests promptly**, particularly cyclone/storm-damage-related issues given the NT's climate.
- [ ] **Keep thorough records** — photos, correspondence, receipts, and condition reports.

## Section 5 — The NT's Tax Advantage (Relevant to Your Overall Compliance Picture)

- [ ] **Remember there's no land tax to manage** — a genuinely lighter ongoing compliance and cost burden compared to every other Australian jurisdiction, worth factoring into your overall investment property management approach.
- [ ] **Focus your compliance energy on tenancy law and safety obligations**, since the tax-side administrative burden is comparatively minimal in the NT.

## Quick Reference — NT Landlord Compliance Calendar

| Obligation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Maximum bond | 4 weeks' rent |
| Rent increase conditions | Clause required, 30 days' notice, none in first 6 months, max once per 6 months |
| Rent bidding/gazumping | Prohibited |
| Rent arrears process | 14 days overdue → 7-day notice → NTCAT application |
| Land tax | None |
