## ⚠️ Important: You're Reading This Right at a Policy Transition Point

Two of Tasmania's biggest first-home-buyer supports were **explicitly time-limited to end on 30 June 2026** — right around when this checklist was written. Here's exactly what was known as at July 2026, and what you need to verify before acting:

- [ ] **The First Home Owner Grant was $30,000 up to 30 June 2026.** A reduced grant of **$20,000 from 1 July 2026** had been **proposed but was subject to legislation** as at the time of writing. **Confirm the current, legislated grant amount directly with the SRO Tasmania before budgeting around any specific figure.**
- [ ] **The 100% stamp duty exemption on established homes (up to $750,000) was confirmed to expire on 30 June 2026, with the SRO explicitly stating it would not be extended.** This means if you're purchasing after this date, you should assume standard transfer duty rates apply to established homes **unless you can confirm a replacement concession has since been legislated.**
- [ ] **The 50% pre-completion duty concession for off-the-plan apartments/units under construction (up to $750,000) was also set to end 30 June 2026** — confirm current status.

**Practical takeaway: don't rely on old headline figures you may have seen elsewhere. This is a genuinely live area — check the SRO Tasmania website directly, ideally with your conveyancer, before making any purchase decision based on assumed grant or duty savings.**

## Section 1 — Get Your Foundations Right (Do this first, before house-hunting)

- [ ] **Check your borrowing capacity with 2–3 lenders or a mortgage broker.**
- [ ] **Confirm the current First Home Owner Grant amount and eligibility directly with the SRO** given the transition described above.
- [ ] **Confirm current stamp duty settings for established homes directly with the SRO** — don't assume either the old exemption or a specific new concession applies without checking.
- [ ] **Check the federal First Home Guarantee** (5% deposit, no LMI) — confirm current Tasmania price caps with a participating lender; this federal scheme operates independently of state-level changes.
- [ ] **Consider the First Home Super Saver Scheme (FHSSS)** if still building your deposit.
- [ ] **Get formal pre-approval, not just an estimate**, given how much your total cost picture depends on confirming current state settings.

## Section 2 — While You're Searching

- [ ] **Set a firm maximum price** based on your pre-approval and current (confirmed) duty settings — don't budget based on savings you haven't verified are still available.
- [ ] **Budget for the possibility that stamp duty relief on established homes may no longer apply** — build a "full duty" scenario into your budget as a safety margin until you've confirmed your specific situation with the SRO.
- [ ] **Budget for costs beyond the purchase price**: conveyancing fees, building and pest inspections, loan fees, and moving costs.

## Section 3 — Know Your Legal Protections Before You Sign

- [ ] **Understand Tasmania has no statutory cooling-off period**, similar to Western Australia. Once you sign a contract of sale, you're bound — there's no window to withdraw.
- [ ] **Make every condition explicit in the contract before you sign** — finance approval, building/pest inspection, and anything else you need must be written into the contract itself.
- [ ] **Get your building and pest inspection arranged before you sign**, or ensure a robust, clearly time-limited inspection condition is built into the contract.
- [ ] **Have a conveyancer or solicitor review the contract before you sign**, not after — critical given there's no cooling-off safety net.

## Section 4 — Between Signing and Settlement

- [ ] **Confirm building insurance timing with your conveyancer.**
- [ ] **Finalise unconditional loan approval** well before settlement.
- [ ] **Book a pre-settlement inspection** in the days before settlement.
- [ ] **Confirm who's lodging your grant and any duty relief paperwork**, and confirm the amounts being applied for match current legislated settings, not outdated figures.

## Section 5 — Common First-Home-Buyer Mistakes to Avoid

- [ ] **Don't assume WA-style "no cooling-off" understanding covers you here** — same rule applies in Tasmania, so treat pre-signature diligence as essential.
- [ ] **Don't budget around headline grant/duty figures without direct confirmation** — this is the single most important caution for Tasmanian first home buyers reading this checklist in mid-to-late 2026.
- [ ] **Don't sign before your inspection and finance conditions are properly documented in the contract.**
