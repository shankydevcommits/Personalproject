# End-of-Lease Bond-Back Checklist — Northern Territory
### Protect your money — most bond disputes are avoidable with the right documentation.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only, not legal advice.*

---

## Section 1 — Understand How Your Bond Works

- [ ] **Know the maximum bond is 4 weeks' rent.**
- [ ] **Understand where your bond is held.** A bond paid to a real estate agent must be placed in a specially designated trust account; a private landlord also holds it in trust, and you can request the bank name and account number.
- [ ] **Know landlords must provide a receipt** for bond paid by cheque, cash, or credit card (electronic bank transfer doesn't require one).
- [ ] **Know a private landlord who's away from the NT for more than 14 days must notify the relevant authority** — a genuinely NT-specific protection given the Territory's transient population patterns.
- [ ] **Understand bond is not a general maintenance fund** — it can only be claimed against for legitimate reasons like unpaid rent or damage beyond fair wear and tear.

## Section 2 — Before You Move Out

- [ ] **Locate your original entry condition report** — your baseline for comparison.
- [ ] **Compare the property's current condition against that report**, room by room.
- [ ] **Clean thoroughly**, addressing anything genuinely beyond fair wear and tear.
- [ ] **Check every item on the original inventory** is present and in working order.
- [ ] **Settle any outstanding rent** before vacating.

## Section 3 — At the Final Inspection

- [ ] **Attend the final inspection if you can**, comparing the property against the original condition report.
- [ ] **Take your own timestamped photos** of every room after cleaning, regardless of whether the agent attends.
- [ ] **Keep every receipt** — cleaning, repairs, pest control — as evidence.
- [ ] **Know the landlord cannot charge you for reasonable wear and tear.**

## Section 4 — Claiming Your Bond

- [ ] **Lodge your side of the bond claim** once you've vacated.
- [ ] **If both parties agree**, the bond is released according to the agreed split.
- [ ] **If there's a dispute**, either party can apply to the **Northern Territory Civil and Administrative Tribunal (NTCAT)** for resolution.

## Section 5 — If There's a Dispute

- [ ] **Gather your evidence before applying to NTCAT**: entry and exit condition reports, timestamped photos, receipts, and correspondence.
- [ ] **Apply to NTCAT** if you and your landlord can't agree — the Tribunal will review evidence from both sides and make a binding decision under the Residential Tenancies Act 1999.
- [ ] **Know landlords generally have limited time to escalate rent arrears matters** — if rent is more than 14 days overdue, the landlord can issue a 7-day notice; if unpaid after that, they may then apply to NTCAT.

---

## Quick Reference — NT Bond Process

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Maximum bond | 4 weeks' rent |
| Bond held in | A designated trust account (agent) or by the landlord in trust |
| Dispute resolution | NTCAT (Northern Territory Civil and Administrative Tribunal) |
| Fair wear and tear | Cannot be charged against your bond |
