# Property Investment Purchase Checklist — Victoria
### Due diligence for buying an investment property, beyond what a homebuyer checklist covers.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only — not financial, tax or legal advice. Property investment involves complex, individual-specific tax and legal considerations; engage a qualified accountant, mortgage broker, and conveyancer before purchasing.*

---

## Section 1 — Structuring the Purchase

- [ ] **Decide on ownership structure before you buy** — individual name, joint ownership, trust, or company each carry different tax, land tax, and asset-protection implications. This decision is very difficult to unwind after settlement, so get advice *before* signing, not after.
- [ ] **Understand how Victorian land tax will apply to this purchase.** Victoria's general land tax threshold was reduced from $300,000 to $50,000 in 2024 — meaning most investment properties in Victoria now attract land tax, even modest-value ones. Your principal place of residence remains exempt, but an investment property generally is not.
- [ ] **Check whether trust-held land applies to you** — trusts face a lower land tax threshold ($25,000 vs $50,000 for individuals) and can trigger surcharge rates unless a principal beneficiary is validly nominated.
- [ ] **If you won't be an Australian tax resident, understand the Absentee Owner Surcharge** — a 4% surcharge on taxable land value applies to absentee owners from the first dollar (no threshold), on top of standard land tax. Notification deadlines to the SRO apply annually (mid-January in recent years) — confirm the current deadline.
- [ ] **Get finance pre-approval specific to investment lending** — investment loans are assessed differently to owner-occupier loans (often slightly higher rates, different serviceability tests), so a standard pre-approval may not reflect your real investment borrowing capacity.

## Section 2 — Due Diligence on the Property Itself

- [ ] **Order a building and pest inspection** — exactly as essential for an investment purchase as an owner-occupied one; undisclosed structural or pest issues erode your yield fast.
- [ ] **Review the Section 32 (Vendor's Statement) carefully**, with particular attention to owners corporation records if buying an apartment/townhouse — special levies or poor sinking fund balances directly affect your investment returns.
- [ ] **Check the Vacant Residential Land Tax (VRLT) exposure if you don't intend to rent it out immediately.** VRLT applies to residential land left vacant for more than 6 months in a calendar year, and (from 1 January 2025) applies across all of Victoria, not just metro Melbourne, at 1% of the capital improved value. If you're buying to renovate before renting, factor this into your timeline.
- [ ] **Research the local rental market specifically** — vacancy rates, typical rents for comparable properties, and tenant demand profile (students, families, professionals) should inform both your purchase decision and your target yield.
- [ ] **Check any short-stay/Airbnb restrictions** if considering short-term letting — Victoria applies a short-stay levy (around 7.5% on stays under 28 days, generally collected by the platform), and owners corporation rules may separately restrict or ban short-stay letting entirely.

## Section 3 — Setting Up for Compliance From Day One

- [ ] **Budget for VIC's rental minimum standards compliance** if the property needs work to meet the 15 mandatory categories (heating, smoke alarms, window coverings, etc. — see the separate Landlord Compliance Checklist in this series for the full list) — factor this into your purchase budget, not as an afterthought post-settlement.
- [ ] **Decide on self-management vs. a property manager** — a property manager typically costs 5–10% of rental income but significantly reduces your compliance risk (condition reports, minimum standards, safety check scheduling, VCAT processes) if you're not experienced in VIC tenancy law.
- [ ] **Arrange landlord insurance** — separate from standard building insurance, this typically covers loss of rent, tenant damage, and liability specific to renting out a property.
- [ ] **Engage a quantity surveyor for a tax depreciation schedule** before or shortly after settlement — see the separate Tax & Depreciation Checklist in this series for what this covers and why timing matters.

## Section 4 — Understanding the Current Tax Environment (Know Before You Buy)

- [ ] **Be aware that negative gearing and CGT settings are changing.** Recent Commonwealth tax reform legislation affects the capital gains tax discount on investment properties, with changes generally applying from 1 July 2027 onward — this is a live policy area, so confirm the current settled position with your accountant before finalising your investment strategy, rather than relying on older assumptions about the 50% CGT discount continuing unchanged indefinitely.
- [ ] **Understand this is a purchase-timing-sensitive area.** Given ongoing reform, the timing of your purchase relative to legislative changeover dates can materially affect your future tax position — this is a conversation to have with your accountant *before* signing, not after.

## Quick Reference — VIC Investment Property Tax Snapshot (July 2026)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Land tax threshold (individuals) | $50,000 (reduced from $300,000 in 2024) |
| Land tax threshold (trusts) | $25,000 |
| Absentee Owner Surcharge | 4% of taxable land value, no threshold |
| Vacant Residential Land Tax | 1% of capital improved value, applies statewide since 1 Jan 2025 |
| Short-stay levy | ~7.5% on stays under 28 days |

*Land tax and surcharge rates change with each state budget — always confirm current figures with the State Revenue Office (sro.vic.gov.au) before settling on a purchase strategy.*
