# Property Investment Purchase Checklist — ACT
### Due diligence for buying an investment property in a leasehold jurisdiction with its own tax model.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only — not financial, tax or legal advice. Engage a qualified accountant, mortgage broker, and conveyancer before purchasing.*

---

## Section 1 — Structuring the Purchase

- [ ] **Decide on ownership structure before you buy** — individual, joint, trust, or company each carry different land tax and asset-protection implications.
- [ ] **Understand the ACT's distinctive tax model.** The ACT has been running a long-term (multi-decade) program shifting away from stamp duty toward broader-based rates — this is why the ACT could fund the 1 July 2026 stamp duty abolition for first home buyers, and it's worth understanding this direction of travel when thinking about your long-term holding costs as an investor.
- [ ] **Know that land tax applies to any ACT residential property that isn't your principal place of residence** — meaning any rental property is generally subject to land tax, in addition to standard rates.
- [ ] **Budget for the foreign ownership surcharge if relevant.** The ACT applies a **7%** foreign investor duty surcharge on residential property purchases by foreign persons.
- [ ] **Note the first-home-buyer stamp duty exemption doesn't apply to you as an investor** — that reform is specifically for owner-occupiers; investors pay standard conveyance duty rates.
- [ ] **Get investment-specific finance pre-approval.**

## Section 2 — Due Diligence on the Property Itself

- [ ] **Order a building and pest inspection** within your cooling-off window or before auction day.
- [ ] **Have a conveyancer or solicitor review the contract before you sign or bid.**
- [ ] **Understand you're purchasing leasehold, not freehold, title** — ask your conveyancer whether the specific Crown lease terms have any implications relevant to renting the property out.
- [ ] **If buying a unit-titled property, review the owners corporation records thoroughly** — sinking fund health and special levies directly affect your investment returns.
- [ ] **Research the local rental market specifically** — vacancy rates, comparable rents, and tenant demand for your target Canberra suburb, noting the significant influence of public service employment patterns on Canberra's rental market.

## Section 3 — Setting Up for Compliance From Day One

- [ ] **Budget for rates and land tax as ongoing costs** — these apply annually and are a genuine, recurring cost of ACT property investment distinct from a one-off stamp duty bill.
- [ ] **Decide on self-management vs. a property manager.**
- [ ] **Arrange landlord insurance**, separate from standard building insurance.
- [ ] **Engage a quantity surveyor for a tax depreciation schedule** shortly after settlement — see the separate Tax & Depreciation Checklist in this series.

## Section 4 — Understanding the Current Tax Environment

- [ ] **Be aware Commonwealth CGT settings for investment property are changing**, with reforms affecting the CGT discount generally applying from **1 July 2027** — confirm the finalised current position with your accountant.
- [ ] **Get advice on purchase and sale timing relative to this changeover** if your investment horizon spans the 2027 transition.

## Quick Reference — ACT Investment Property Snapshot (July 2026)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Land tax | Applies to any non-principal-place-of-residence property |
| Rates | Apply to all ACT properties, funding essential services |
| Foreign ownership surcharge (duty) | 7% |
| First home buyer stamp duty exemption | Does not apply to investors |
| Land tenure | Leasehold (Crown lease), not freehold |

*Land tax and rates settings change with territory budgets — always confirm current figures with the ACT Revenue Office before settling on a purchase strategy.*
