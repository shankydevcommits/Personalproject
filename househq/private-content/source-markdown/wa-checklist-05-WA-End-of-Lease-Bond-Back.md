# End-of-Lease Bond-Back Checklist — Western Australia
### Protect your money — recent reforms changed how bond disputes are handled.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only, not legal advice.*

---

## Section 1 — Understand How Your Bond Works (Recently Changed)

- [ ] **Know about the 2026 bond release reforms.** Following the Residential Tenancies Amendment Act 2024, significant changes to the bond release process commenced on **28 March 2026** — the process is designed to be more streamlined and transparent than the previous system.
- [ ] **Know any party can now initiate bond release.** Previously only the owner or property manager could start this process — as of the 2026 reforms, the tenant can also initiate a bond release request.
- [ ] **Understand bond disputes are no longer automatically sent to the Magistrates Court.** Disputes are now handled through a Commissioner-based process first.
- [ ] **Know that fair wear and tear cannot be claimed against your bond.**

## Section 2 — Before You Move Out

- [ ] **Locate your original entry condition report** — your baseline for comparison.
- [ ] **Compare the property's current condition against that report**, room by room.
- [ ] **Clean thoroughly**, addressing anything genuinely beyond fair wear and tear.
- [ ] **Check every item on the original inventory** is present and in working order.
- [ ] **Settle any outstanding rent** before vacating.

## Section 3 — At the Final Inspection

- [ ] **Attend the final inspection if you can**, comparing the property against the original condition report.
- [ ] **Take your own timestamped photos** of every room after cleaning, regardless of whether the agent attends.
- [ ] **Keep every receipt** — cleaning, repairs, pest control — as evidence.

## Section 4 — Claiming Your Bond

- [ ] **Initiate the bond release process yourself if needed** — this is now an option available to tenants directly under the 2026 reforms, not just the landlord/agent.
- [ ] **If both parties agree**, the bond is released according to the agreed split.
- [ ] **If there's a disagreement**, the matter goes through the Commissioner-based determination process first.
- [ ] **Know your appeal rights.** If you disagree with a bond determination, you can appeal to the **Magistrates Court within 7 days** of the decision.

## Section 5 — If There's a Dispute

- [ ] **Gather your evidence before lodging a dispute**: entry and exit condition reports, timestamped photos, receipts, and correspondence.
- [ ] **Be aware the new process has had mixed early feedback.** Since the reforms only commenced in March 2026, some cases have experienced delays in determinations, and once a matter is referred to the Commissioner, there's generally no further opportunity for negotiation between parties — so try to resolve informally first if there's any real prospect of agreement.
- [ ] **If you disagree with the outcome, act quickly** — the 7-day appeal window to the Magistrates Court is tight.

## Quick Reference — WA Bond Process (Post-March 2026 Reforms)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Bond release reform | Commenced 28 March 2026 (Residential Tenancies Amendment Act 2024) |
| Who can initiate release | Any party — tenant, owner, or managing agent |
| Dispute resolution | Commissioner-based process, not automatic Magistrates Court referral |
| Appeal window | 7 days to the Magistrates Court |
| Maximum pet bond | $350 |
| Fair wear and tear | Cannot be claimed against bond |
