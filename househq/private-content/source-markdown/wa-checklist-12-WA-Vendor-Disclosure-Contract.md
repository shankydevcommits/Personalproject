# Vendor Disclosure & Contract Checklist — Western Australia
### Your obligations as a seller — get this right, because there's no cooling-off period to smooth over gaps.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only, not legal advice. Your contract of sale should be prepared with a settlement agent or solicitor — this checklist helps you gather what they'll need, not replace their advice.*

---

## Section 1 — Understanding Your Position as a WA Vendor

- [ ] **Know that WA has no statutory cooling-off period for either party.** This makes accurate, complete disclosure and a properly prepared contract especially important — once your buyer signs, there's no grace period during which incomplete information can be quietly corrected without risk.
- [ ] **Understand your general disclosure obligations.** While WA's disclosure framework differs in structure from states with a dedicated statutory "vendor statement" regime, sellers still have obligations to avoid misleading or deceptive conduct, and known material defects should be disclosed to avoid later disputes or claims.
- [ ] **Work with your settlement agent or solicitor to prepare the contract properly before marketing begins** — given there's no cooling-off buffer, a well-prepared contract reduces the risk of disputes arising after a binding signature.

## Section 2 — Information to Gather for Your Settlement Agent/Solicitor

- [ ] **Title details and any encumbrances** — mortgages, easements, covenants affecting the land.
- [ ] **Zoning and planning information.**
- [ ] **Known building work history and any relevant approvals.**
- [ ] **Strata records**, if applicable — recent meeting minutes, financials, sinking fund details, and known disputes or special levies.
- [ ] **Any known defects or issues** you're aware of, even if not immediately obvious — full disclosure protects you from post-settlement disputes.

## Section 3 — Underquoting and Price Transparency

- [ ] **Discuss price guide practices with your agent.** While WA's specific regulatory framework around price representations differs from some eastern states, misleading price conduct can still raise consumer protection issues — ask your agent how they'll represent price expectations to prospective buyers.
- [ ] **Don't ask your agent to advertise a misleadingly low price to generate interest.**

## Section 4 — Auction and Private Treaty Considerations

- [ ] **Understand there's no cooling-off period regardless of sale method** in WA — this differs from most eastern states where auctions specifically lack cooling-off but private treaty sales have one.
- [ ] **Ensure your contract is genuinely complete and accurate before marketing begins**, since both auction and private treaty buyers will be immediately bound upon signing.

## Section 5 — Final Checks Before Going to Market

- [ ] **Confirm your contract of sale is complete and reviewed by your settlement agent/solicitor** before your first open home.
- [ ] **Cross-check the contract against your own knowledge of the property** — flag any inconsistency with your settlement agent/solicitor immediately.
- [ ] **Keep a copy of everything provided to buyers.**
