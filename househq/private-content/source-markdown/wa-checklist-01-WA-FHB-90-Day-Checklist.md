# The First Home Buyer's 90-Day Checklist — Western Australia
### Everything you need to sort before you sign, in plain English.

*Correct as at July 2026. Grant amounts and thresholds are set by government and can change — always confirm current details with RevenueWA (wa.gov.au) before relying on this for a purchase decision. This checklist is general information, not financial or legal advice.*

---

## Section 1 — Get Your Foundations Right (Do this first, before house-hunting)

- [ ] **Check your borrowing capacity with 2–3 lenders or a mortgage broker.**
- [ ] **Check your eligibility for the WA First Home Owner Grant.** Currently **$10,000** for new homes and substantially renovated homes — **not available for established homes**. Following the May 2026 State Budget, the property price cap increased from **$750,000 to $800,000** (for homes south of the 26th parallel — confirm the applicable cap for your specific location, as regional/northern WA settings can differ).
- [ ] **Understand the stamp duty (transfer duty) exemption — this applies to both new and established homes.** Following the 7 May 2026 budget changes: **full exemption up to $600,000** (increased from $500,000), with a **sliding-scale concession up to $800,000** (increased from $700,000).
- [ ] **Check vacant land thresholds if building.** The exemption threshold rose from $350,000 to **$450,000**, with concessional rates extended to **$550,000**.
- [ ] **Consider the off-the-plan concession if buying pre-construction.** Up to 100% duty relief is available, extended by two years to **30 June 2028**, with more generous terms for eligible contracts entered into on or after 12 March 2026.
- [ ] **Look into Keystart**, WA's state-run low-deposit home loan scheme (around 2% deposit) — separate from the FHOG, and worth comparing against a mainstream lender using the federal First Home Guarantee, as Keystart isn't automatically your best or only option.
- [ ] **Check the federal First Home Guarantee** (5% deposit, no LMI) — confirm current Perth/regional WA price caps with a participating lender.
- [ ] **Get formal pre-approval, not just an estimate.**

## Section 2 — While You're Searching

- [ ] **Set a firm maximum price** based on your pre-approval.
- [ ] **Budget for costs beyond the purchase price**: settlement agent/conveyancer fees, building and pest inspections, loan fees, and moving costs.
- [ ] **Understand WA uses "settlement agents" (or solicitors) rather than "conveyancers" in common usage** — functionally similar, but confirm your chosen provider is licensed to act in WA.

## Section 3 — Know Your Legal Protections Before You Sign (This Is Different in WA)

- [ ] **Understand WA has no statutory cooling-off period at all.** Unlike most other states, once you sign a contract of sale in Western Australia, you are immediately and fully committed — there is no window to change your mind, whether buying at auction or through private treaty.
- [ ] **Make every condition explicit in the contract before you sign** — finance approval, building and pest inspection, and any other condition you need must be written into the contract itself, since there's no cooling-off safety net to fall back on if something goes wrong after signing.
- [ ] **Get your building and pest inspection arranged and, ideally, completed before you sign**, or ensure a robust inspection condition with a clear timeframe is built into the contract.
- [ ] **Have a settlement agent or solicitor review the contract before you sign**, not after — in WA, this review needs to happen pre-signature given the lack of a cooling-off period.

## Section 4 — Between Signing and Settlement

- [ ] **Confirm building insurance timing with your settlement agent/solicitor.**
- [ ] **Finalise unconditional loan approval** well before settlement.
- [ ] **Book a pre-settlement inspection** in the days before settlement.
- [ ] **Lodge your FHOG application with your home loan application** — don't wait until after settlement; WA guidance specifically recommends applying alongside your loan, and you can also apply directly through the WA Department of Finance within 12 months of settlement if needed.

## Section 5 — Common First-Home-Buyer Mistakes to Avoid

- [ ] **Don't assume you have a cooling-off period** — this is the single biggest difference between WA and most other Australian states, and it catches buyers out who assume a "standard" grace period applies.
- [ ] **Don't sign before your inspection and finance conditions are properly documented in the contract.**
- [ ] **Don't assume the FHOG applies to established homes** — it's new/substantially renovated homes only; the stamp duty exemption is what applies to established homes.
- [ ] **Don't assume Keystart is automatically your best option** — compare it against mainstream lenders using the First Home Guarantee.

## Quick Reference — Western Australia First Home Buyer Support (July 2026)

| Support | Amount / Detail | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| First Home Owner Grant | $10,000 | New/substantially renovated homes, cap $800,000 (south of 26th parallel) |
| Stamp duty exemption | $0 duty | New or established homes, ≤$600,000 |
| Stamp duty concession | Sliding scale | $600,000–$800,000 |
| Vacant land exemption | $0 duty | ≤$450,000 |
| Off-the-plan concession | Up to 100% relief | Extended to 30 June 2028 |
| Cooling-off period | **None** | No statutory right to withdraw after signing |

*Sources: RevenueWA (wa.gov.au), WA Department of Finance. Always verify current figures before making a purchase decision.*
