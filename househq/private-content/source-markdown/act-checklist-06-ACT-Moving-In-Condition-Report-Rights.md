# Moving-In Condition Report & Rights Checklist — ACT
### Get this right on day one — it protects your bond for the entire tenancy.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only, not legal advice.*

---

## Section 1 — Before You Sign the Lease

- [ ] **Know your tenancy is governed by the Residential Tenancies Act 1997 (ACT)** and the Residential Tenancies Regulation 1998.
- [ ] **Ask about heating quality specifically** — given Canberra's cold winters, functioning, efficient heating is a genuine liveability issue worth confirming before you sign, not just a nice-to-have.
- [ ] **Ask about smoke alarm compliance** and any recent safety checks.

## Section 2 — The Condition Report Itself

- [ ] **Request the entry condition report** if not automatically provided.
- [ ] **Complete your own section thoroughly**, room by room.
- [ ] **Photograph everything**, especially existing wear, damage, or imperfections.
- [ ] **Note existing issues explicitly.**
- [ ] **Test everything that should work**: taps, toilets, switches, power points, stovetop, heating, and any included appliances.
- [ ] **Submit your condition report within the required timeframe.**
- [ ] **Keep a copy for yourself** — you'll need it again at the end of your lease.

## Section 3 — Ongoing Rights During Your Tenancy

- [ ] **Know your rent increase protections.** Increases require at least **8 weeks' written notice**, and **cannot occur within the first 12 months** of your tenancy — genuinely stronger initial protection than several other states.
- [ ] **Know you can challenge an increase.** If you believe a rent increase is excessive, you can raise this with your landlord and, if unresolved, apply to ACAT.
- [ ] **Request repairs in writing** — this creates a paper trail if a dispute later needs to go to ACAT.
- [ ] **Know your bond can't exceed 4 weeks' rent.**

## Section 4 — Red Flags at Move-In

- [ ] **A condition report with suspiciously few noted issues** on an older or clearly lived-in property.
- [ ] **Being pressured to sign before receiving the condition report or properly inspecting.**
- [ ] **A property with genuinely inadequate heating** given Canberra's climate — this affects both comfort and, potentially, your ongoing energy costs.

## Quick Reference — ACT Tenant Rights & Key Facts

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Governing legislation | Residential Tenancies Act 1997 (ACT) |
| Rent increase notice | 8 weeks, none within first 12 months |
| Maximum bond | 4 weeks' rent |
| Dispute resolution | ACAT (ACT Civil and Administrative Tribunal) |
