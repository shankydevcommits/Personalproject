# End-of-Lease Bond-Back Checklist — South Australia
### Protect your money — most bond disputes are avoidable with the right documentation.

*Correct as at July 2026. General information only, not legal advice.*

---

## Section 1 — Understand How Your Bond Works

- [ ] **Know your bond is lodged with the Residential Tenancies Fund**, not held by your landlord or agent directly.
- [ ] **Know the lodgement deadlines that apply to whoever manages your tenancy** — bonds must generally be lodged within 2 weeks (self-managing landlords) or 4 weeks (registered agents) of receipt, which affects how quickly your bond is properly protected from the start of your tenancy.
- [ ] **Understand bond is not a general maintenance fund** — it can only be claimed against for unpaid rent, cleaning to restore the property (allowing for fair wear and tear), or damage beyond fair wear and tear.

## Section 2 — Before You Move Out

- [ ] **Locate your original entry condition report** — your baseline for comparison.
- [ ] **Compare the property's current condition against that report**, room by room.
- [ ] **Clean thoroughly**, addressing anything genuinely beyond fair wear and tear.
- [ ] **Check every item on the original inventory** is present and in working order.
- [ ] **Settle any outstanding rent** before vacating.

## Section 3 — At the Final Inspection

- [ ] **Attend the final inspection if you can**, comparing the property against the original condition report.
- [ ] **Take your own timestamped photos** of every room after cleaning, regardless of whether the agent attends.
- [ ] **Keep every receipt** — cleaning, repairs, pest control — as evidence.

## Section 4 — Claiming Your Bond

- [ ] **Lodge your side of the bond claim** once you've vacated.
- [ ] **If both parties agree**, the bond is released according to the agreed split.
- [ ] **If there's a dispute**, either party can apply to the **South Australian Civil and Administrative Tribunal (SACAT)** for resolution.
- [ ] **Know about the evidence requirements introduced in the 2024 reforms.** Bond claims now face stricter evidence rules, designed to eliminate unreliable claims — meaning both you and your landlord need to substantiate claims with genuine documentation, not just assertions.

## Section 5 — If There's a Dispute

- [ ] **Gather your evidence before applying to SACAT**: entry and exit condition reports, timestamped photos, receipts, and correspondence.
- [ ] **Know about internal review limits.** Following the 2024 reforms, a party generally cannot apply for internal review of a SACAT order requiring payment of money unless exceptional circumstances apply — this is designed to prevent delays in compensation and bond payments, so treat your SACAT hearing as your main opportunity to present your case thoroughly.
- [ ] **Apply promptly** if you disagree with an outcome, given the limited review pathway.

## Quick Reference — SA Bond Process

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Bond lodgement deadline | 2 weeks (self-managing landlord) / 4 weeks (agent) |
| Dispute resolution | SACAT (South Australian Civil and Administrative Tribunal) |
| Evidence requirements | Strengthened under 2024 reforms |
| Internal review of money orders | Limited to exceptional circumstances (since 2024) |
| Fair wear and tear | Cannot be charged against your bond |
